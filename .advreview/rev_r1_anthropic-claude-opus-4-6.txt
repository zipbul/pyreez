## Finding 1: Self-Contradictory Instruction in Round 2 Bullet 4

**steelman:** The "do not pad" clause prevents low-value output when no new critique survives, preserving token budget and signal density.

**weakness:** The sentence immediately continues: "pick the peer finding you judge weakest and try to refute it." This is a mandatory busywork assignment that directly contradicts "do not pad." If no new substantive critique survived the model's counter-attack, it has no strong evidence against any peer finding — yet the instruction forces it to attack the weakest one anyway, producing exactly the padding the first clause prohibits. The model must resolve a within-sentence contradiction, and different models will resolve it differently (some will pad, some will skip), breaking consistency across the 3-model panel.

**evidence:** The full text: *"If none does, do not pad — pick the peer finding you judge weakest and try to refute it."* "Do not pad" = don't generate low-value content. "Pick the weakest and try to refute it" = generate content even without strong evidence. These are logically contradictory when the model genuinely has no counter-evidence. The instruction also conflates "weakest peer finding" (lowest quality, most worth dropping) with "finding the model can most easily refute" (which may be the strongest, not weakest).

**falsification:** Run round 2 with three models where no new critique survives. Check whether outputs for the mandatory-refutation segment are (a) substantive or (b) padding. If >1 of 3 produce padding, the contradiction is confirmed to leak.

**verdict:** high severity · HIGH confidence

## Finding 2: "exact recall" Permission Contradicts Bullet 5 Downgrade

**steelman:** Bullet 1 permits two evidence sources — reasoning chains and exact recall — to avoid hamstringing the model. Bullet 5 then adds a review pass to catch unreliable memory.

**weakness:** Bullet 1 says *"reasoning chains (mechanism → break → consequence) or exact recall only"* — establishing "exact recall" as a first-class, permitted evidence source, equal in status to reasoning chains. Bullet 5 then says *"Before submitting, downgrade any source/number/quote held by memory alone."* Every "exact recall" citation is by definition "held by memory alone," so bullet 5 universally downgrades the category bullet 1 authorized. The model receives two signals: (a) exact recall is a valid evidence class, (b) anything from memory must be downgraded. A compliant model that follows both bullets will never present memory-sourced evidence at HIGH confidence, which is correct — but a model that weighs bullet 1 more heavily will skip the downgrade, and a model that weighs bullet 5 more heavily will avoid citing recall at all, losing legitimate evidence. The net effect depends on model-specific instruction priority, breaking cross-model consistency.

**evidence:** Logical entailment: "exact recall" ⊂ "held by memory alone." Therefore bullet 5's downgrade universally applies to bullet 1's second permitted source. The two bullets encode the same content as "you may use memory, but always at reduced confidence" — which could be stated once without the contradictory framing.

**falsification:** Prompt a weak model (e.g., a smaller open-weight model in the panel) with a proposal containing a well-known factual hook. Check whether it cites the fact at HIGH confidence (bullet 1 wins), LOW/[unverified] (bullet 5 wins), or avoids citing it entirely (bullet 5 over-applied). Any divergence from the intended behavior confirms the tension matters.

**verdict:** medium severity · HIGH confidence

## Finding 3: "reason before each verdict" Is Redundant with Field Ordering

**steelman:** The phrase ensures analytical rigor — the model should think before judging.

**weakness:** The output format specifies this exact field order: steelman → weakness → evidence → falsification → **verdict**. The reasoning (weakness + evidence) already structurally precedes the verdict by field ordering. The clause *"reason before each verdict"* either (a) restates what the field order already enforces (pure redundancy, wasting cached tokens every round), or (b) asks for *additional* free-text reasoning before the verdict field that isn't captured in the defined fields (ambiguous — where does it go?). Either reading is a defect: redundancy or under-specification.

**evidence:** The field order list places `evidence` and `weakness` before `verdict`. Any model following field order will reason before verdicting. The clause adds no behavioral delta under interpretation (a) and creates an undefined structural slot under interpretation (b).

**falsification:** Delete *"reason before each verdict"* and run 6 responses. If output structure and verdict quality are unchanged, redundancy is confirmed.

**verdict:** low severity · HIGH confidence

## Finding 4: Partial Redundancy — "keep the rest" (Role) vs. "Keep low-confidence findings" (Bullet 3)

**steelman:** The role block and the confidence definitions serve different functions (task-setting vs. calibration rules), so restating the "keep low-confidence findings" norm in each is belt-and-suspenders.

**weakness:** The role says *"keep the rest — weak-but-plausible ones at low confidence."* Bullet 3 says *"Keep low-confidence findings; never inflate."* The first half of bullet 3's sentence ("Keep low-confidence findings") is a verbatim restatement of the role's instruction. Only "never inflate" adds new content. In a prompt where every redundant line is re-sent each round and dilutes attention (the stated stake), this duplication costs tokens and competes for attention with unique content. The "never inflate" clause could be merged into the role's sentence or kept standalone without the redundant prefix.

**evidence:** Role: "keep the rest — weak-but-plausible ones at low confidence." Bullet 3: "Keep low-confidence findings." Same directive, same semantic content, two locations in the cached system block.

**falsification:** Rewrite bullet 3 as *"Never inflate confidence."* (4 tokens vs. 11). Run 6 responses. If low-confidence findings still appear, the "Keep" prefix was redundant.

**verdict:** low severity · HIGH confidence

## Finding 5: "Enumerate … drop" Assumes Internal Scratchpad

**steelman:** The instruction to enumerate failure modes, attack each, and drop defeated ones is a quality filter that prevents the output from containing self-refuted critiques.

**weakness:** The role says: *"Enumerate candidate failure modes, attack each, drop any your own counter-attack defeats."* This is a generate-then-filter procedure. In autoregressive models without an explicit thinking/scratchpad phase, the model cannot enumerate and then drop — it can only choose not to emit. Models with extended thinking (Claude's thinking blocks, OpenAI's chain-of-thought) can perform this internally, but the prompt doesn't know which models are in the 3-model panel. For models without scratchpad, the instruction either (a) is ignored (the model just emits surviving findings directly, which is fine but means the instruction is dead weight) or (b) is followed literally, producing a visible list of failure modes with some marked "dropped" — wasting output tokens on content the prompt told it to exclude.

**evidence:** Autoregressive generation is left-to-right; once tokens are emitted, they cannot be retracted. "Drop" requires either pre-generation planning (thinking mode) or post-generation editing (not available). The prompt is model-agnostic but the instruction is architecture-dependent.

**falsification:** Run the prompt on a model without extended thinking (e.g., a standard completion model). Check whether it (a) emits then drops, (b) silently filters, or (c) ignores the instruction. If (a), the instruction wastes tokens; if (c), it's dead weight.

**verdict:** medium severity · MEDIUM confidence

## Finding 6: No Output-Length Constraint Risks Context Blowup

**steelman:** Leaving output unconstrained lets the model produce as many findings as warranted, avoiding artificial truncation of valid critique.

**weakness:** The protocol is multi-round: each model's output from round N becomes peer context in round N+1. With 3 models × unconstrained output, the peer context can grow super-linearly (each model responds to all peers, adding new and revised findings). The system block is cached and re-sent every round, so total context = cached system + accumulated peer outputs + user block. Without any output budget guidance (even a soft one like "aim for ≤ N findings" or "≤ K tokens"), a verbose model can exhaust the context window by round 2-3, or push earlier findings into the lost-in-the-middle zone where they receive less attention. This is especially dangerous because the prompt's own stake section identifies lost-in-the-middle as the key risk — yet addresses it only for the system block, not for the output.

**evidence:** The prompt's stake section: *"Every redundant line is re-sent each round and dilutes attention (lost-in-the-middle)."* This correctly identifies the risk for the system prompt but applies no mitigation to model output, which accumulates across rounds and is equally subject to the same attention-dilution effect.

**falsification:** Measure total context length at round 3 across the 3 models. If any model's input context exceeds 80% of its window, or if findings from round 1 fall in the middle third of the context, the leak is confirmed.

**verdict:** medium severity · MEDIUM confidence

## Finding 7: Round 1 "substantive and falsifiable" Partially Duplicated by Format Fields

**steelman:** The approach line *"Every finding must be substantive and falsifiable"* sets a quality floor that the structured fields (evidence, falsification) can't enforce alone — a field can be filled with trivial content.

**weakness:** The output format already requires an `evidence` field (enforcing substantiveness — you must provide a reasoning chain) and a `falsification` field (enforcing falsifiability — you must name a test). While the approach line sets intent, the structured fields enforce it mechanically. Removing the approach line and relying on the fields may produce equivalent output. However, this is lower-confidence because the approach line plausibly raises the bar above "fill in the field with anything," so the behavioral delta may be nonzero.

**evidence:** `evidence` field enforces: "your reasoning chain, or an exact-recall citation" — a finding without substance can't fill this. `falsification` field enforces: "the cheapest concrete test that would change your mind" — a non-falsifiable finding can't fill this. The approach line's "substantive and falsifiable" requirement is therefore structurally enforced by the format.

**falsification:** Delete the approach line from round 1, run 6 responses, compare substantiveness and falsification quality. If no degradation, the line was redundant.

**verdict:** low severity · MEDIUM confidence

---

The prompt is acceptable when: (1) the round 2 bullet 4 contradiction ("do not pad" vs. mandatory weakest-peer refutation) is resolved — either drop the forced refutation or reframe it as optional; (2) the "exact recall" / downgrade tension is collapsed into a single clear statement (e.g., "Use reasoning chains as primary evidence; memory-sourced facts are permitted but must be marked [unverified] and capped at MEDIUM confidence"); and (3) the "reason before each verdict" clause and the "Keep low-confidence findings" prefix are deleted as redundant with existing structural and role-level instructions.
