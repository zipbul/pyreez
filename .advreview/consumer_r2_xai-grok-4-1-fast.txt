**Finding 1**

**target:** Analyst B

**steelman:** PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE risks are manageable with `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`, `lock_timeout`, and additive-only steps; a 4-person team can follow documented patterns and avoid the ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock queue that caused GoCardless downtime.

**weakness:** Even with additive discipline, the first production pivot that requires a type change or NOT NULL on an existing column (common when early customer data reveals an enum that must become a foreign key) still acquires ACCESS EXCLUSIVE. On a 6-month deadline the team has no buffer to implement pg_osc or shadow-table patterns under load; the resulting queue blocks every SELECT for the duration of the table rewrite, directly delaying the MVP launch window.

**evidence:** Xata and DEV Community sources confirm `SET NOT NULL` and type changes require full scans or rewrites under ACCESS EXCLUSIVE; the mechanism is deterministic regardless of team size.

**falsification:** Review the actual first ten migration scripts after month one; if none contain a non-additive operation, the lock-queue exposure claim is refuted.

**verdict:** high | HIGH

**Finding 2**

**target:** Analyst A

**steelman:** JSONB + GIN gives document flexibility inside Postgres while preserving transactions; the dual-schema burden only appears when JSONB dominates.

**weakness:** Under sustained partial updates to large documents (feature flags, activity timelines, integration payloads), MVCC still rewrites the entire heap tuple and all GIN index entries on every change. A 4-person team without prior autovacuum or bloat monitoring experience will accumulate dead tuples faster than they can diagnose, producing production query degradation inside the first 60 days.

**evidence:** The documented MVCC full-row copy plus GIN decomposition cost is confirmed by the AWS Builders and sitepoint sources; the operational detection burden falls on generalists who lack `pg_stat_user_tables` or `pgstattuple` experience.

**falsification:** After four weeks of production load, run `SELECT * FROM pg_stat_user_tables WHERE n_dead_tup > 0.1 * n_live_tup`; if the ratio stays below 10 %, the bloat claim is refuted.

**verdict:** medium | MEDIUM

**Finding 3**

**target:** Analyst B

**steelman:** Connection exhaustion only occurs under serverless burst patterns; a monolithic deployment with a single pooler keeps Postgres within default `max_connections = 100`.

**weakness:** Even a long-lived process deployment will hit exhaustion during the first marketing-driven traffic spike when background jobs, health checks, and user requests coincide; without a pre-configured external pooler the 4-person team must add, tune, and validate PgBouncer or Supavisor under time pressure, risking launch slippage.

**evidence:** The loke.dev scenario and PostgreSQL docs on `max_connections` sizing confirm the architectural limit exists independent of managed service; the setup cost remains on the team.

**falsification:** Instrument connection count in the first production week; if it never exceeds 60 concurrent, the exhaustion path is refuted.

**verdict:** low | MEDIUM

Analyst B Finding 5 is the weakest: its operational-tuning tax is almost entirely team-experience dependent and adds no new falsifiable production failure mode beyond the migration and connection findings already surfaced. It is refuted.

PostgreSQL is acceptable only when the team can produce a finalized ER diagram before month 1, commits exclusively to additive migrations with `lock_timeout`, and designates one engineer to own connection-pool and vacuum monitoring from day one.
