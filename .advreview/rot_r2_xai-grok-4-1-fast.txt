Finding 1
target: Analyst A
steelman: PostgreSQL logical replication and read replicas are mature, yet unplanned multi-region or 10x write scaling by month 5 requires either Citus or application-level sharding that consumes weeks of DevOps effort a four-person team cannot absorb, whereas MongoDB sharding is a configuration change.
weakness: The scenario collapses under adversarial customer input received at month 4: a single enterprise prospect demands contractual data residency in two sovereign jurisdictions plus write durability guarantees that force immediate geographic replication. PostgreSQL’s read-only replicas plus Citus introduce foreign-key and transaction boundary failures across shards that must be resolved before the contract can be signed; MongoDB replica sets with zone sharding satisfy the residency clauses in days. The six-month MVP deadline therefore converts an “unplanned scaling” item into a lost deal rather than postponed infrastructure work.
evidence: Logical replication preserves foreign-key semantics only within a single region; crossing regions requires either two-phase commit emulation or relaxed consistency that the customer explicitly rejects in the RFP, whereas MongoDB zone sharding encodes residency directly in the shard key without altering application transaction boundaries.
falsification: Present the identical residency clause to both stacks in a one-week contract simulation and measure calendar days until a signed, production-feasible deployment exists; equality falsifies the weakness.
verdict: high MEDIUM

Finding 2
target: Analyst B
steelman: When the unit of work is a single tenant-scoped aggregate, MongoDB avoids relational impedance mismatch and the team ships faster.
weakness: Adversarial data import arrives at month 5 in the form of a customer CSV containing historical multi-tenant records that must be merged under existing unique constraints and foreign-key relationships already enforced for compliance reporting. PostgreSQL accepts the import inside a single transaction that aborts on any violation; MongoDB requires application-level reconciliation scripts whose correctness cannot be proven before the 6-month launch, producing either data loss or a missed regulatory checkpoint.
evidence: A relational engine validates uniqueness and referential integrity at ingest time without extra code; a document store can only surface conflicts after separate aggregation queries, shifting the validation burden onto the four-person team already at capacity.
falsification: Execute the exact customer CSV import on both schemas and record whether any integrity violation is rejected before the data become visible to the application; zero undetected violations falsifies the weakness.
verdict: medium MEDIUM

Finding 3
target: Analyst A
steelman: JSONB plus GIN indexes plus modern ORMs narrow the gap for nested domain structures so that PostgreSQL remains viable.
weakness: An adversarial product requirement surfaces at month 3.5: the collaborative feature must support arbitrary per-customer plug-in schemas that mutate after documents are already persisted, while preserving atomic append-only history for legal audit. PostgreSQL’s JSONB forces either repeated partial-index rebuilds or a separate audit table whose foreign keys break when the plug-in schema changes; MongoDB accepts schema-per-document plus built-in change streams that emit the audit trail without index churn.
evidence: GIN index maintenance on a mutating JSONB column triggers full re-indexing on any key addition, whereas MongoDB maintains per-document indexes and change streams without global lock or rebuild.
falsification: Load 10 000 documents with three successive plug-in schema extensions and compare p99 write latency and index size growth; parity falsifies the weakness.
verdict: medium LOW
